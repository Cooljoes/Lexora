Britfone includes IPA transcriptions, with stress attached to vowel tokens. Its
author credits CMUdict, Wiktionary, Wikipedia, Collins, Oxford, Cambridge and
Macmillan for initial transcriptions or British specifics. Lexora uses the
published Britfone dataset and retains its accent-specific phonemes.

The Carnegie Mellon Pronouncing Dictionary • Carnegie Mellon University
https://github.com/cmusphinx/cmudict/tree/74790861f652b15e4ac49015a90074ad62a27690
Source: cmudict.dict • SHA-256 81917843c7f44ce2b094ac63873c2c7a4cf802040792c455ba3ca406891c3d22
CMUdict records North American pronunciations in ARPAbet. Lexora converts
those recorded phonemes to broad US IPA. For example, AH0 and ER0 are rendered
as /ə/ and /ɚ/, stressed AH and ER as /ʌ/ and /ɝ/, and R as /ɹ/. Vowel length
and accent details beyond the source transcription are not inferred.

Both reference dictionaries index spellings. Alternate pronunciations are
retained in source order, deduplicated after notation changes, without
assigning a pronunciation to a part of speech or meaning. Both sources attach
stress to the vowel. In the app, single-syllable forms have no stress mark.
For longer forms, stress stays on its original syllable: its mark is moved to
the start of the word or the longest permitted following-syllable onset.
This is a display convention; neither source supplies a full syllabification.
The mapping and onset rules are in Tools/import_pronunciations.py.
